# Glucose — Structure & Reactions

## Introduction
Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is the most abundant monosaccharide and the primary energy source for cells. It exists in both open-chain and cyclic forms.

[CONCEPT: Key Properties | Molecular formula: C₆H₁₂O₆ ;; Contains one aldehyde group at C1 ;; Six hydroxyl groups (5 in ring + 1 terminal) ;; Strongly reducing sugar — reduces Tollens and Fehling reagents ;; Sweet in taste, soluble in water]

## Structure

The open-chain form has an aldehyde at C1 and six carbons total. In aqueous solution, >99% exists as the cyclic pyranose form.

## Reactions
| Reagent | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Br₂ water | Gluconic acid | Aldehyde present (mild oxidation) |
| HNO₃ | Saccharic acid | Both CHO and primary OH present |
| HI (conc.) | n-Hexane | All 6 carbons in straight chain |
| HCN | Cyanohydrin | Aldehyde group confirmed |
| Acetic anhydride | Penta-acetate | Five OH groups |
| CH₃OH / HCl | Methyl glucoside | Cyclic structure exists |

[TRAP: Sucrose is NON-reducing because both anomeric carbons are involved in the glycosidic bond — unlike glucose which freely reduces Tollens reagent.]
